- Keychain use-case: on commute, with haptic feedback; discussed when and where it would be used.
- In emotionally close relationships, privacy is much more complex than in general use cases.

- Agreed the keychain should not encourage or create a power dynamic between partners.

**Group discussion:**

- Feature idea: a word-defining emoji pops up.
- Feature idea: blocking negative energy.
- Have a preset message to go alongside the face.

**Feedback from Rosti:**

- As long as the project can demonstrate social mobile computing, that's the main bar.
- Ethical issues: be aware of what the design creates, then design with that awareness — doesn't need to be "solved," just designed in good faith.
- A self-reflection feature aimed at an "abuser" won't fix the underlying problem, since they're unlikely to acknowledge it themselves.
- Address this honestly as a design constraint rather than claiming to solve it.
- Use an ethical framework to help identify issues, even if it doesn't eliminate them.
- The product doesn't need to resolve abuse — but it shouldn't promote it either.

## 8 September 2026 — Tuesday In-Class Meeting

### Activities and Discussion

- Miro board activity exploring three lenses: Slow Technology, Tangible Interaction, and Speculative Design.
- Went over the primary research (iteration 2) analysis and mapped out themes and design direction.
- Ideated on the keychain's look and form.
- Our product connects most naturally to Slow Technology and Tangible Interaction.
- User needs = design implication here, since users often don't know exactly what they need, but they do know what they struggle with.
- **Feature 1 — Keychain:** mobile, low-stakes/not too serious; fits Slow Technology, since mood and emotion often need time to register or process.
- **Feature 2 — Mood notes (optional):** primary research showed partners can usually tell something's off, but not *why* — this feature gives a lightweight way to express that.
- Matched themes across the research to the keychain + app pairing; agreed each theme's design ideas should carry through to the app as well, not just the keychain.

### Key Decisions / Justification

- **Keychain form:** go with something that can show different faces at keychain scale — enough to communicate the prototype idea to participants, doesn't need to be polished.
- **Moods selected:** happy, sad, tired, grateful, stressed, energised — with an equal number of positive and negative moods, so the design doesn't encourage either.
- **Theme 1** is the only theme implemented on the physical product.
- **Themes 1–5 (excluding 4)** carry into the app.
- **Theme 4 dropped (for now):** "prompted care" risks feeling non-genuine rather than authentic.

## 27 August 2026 — Thursday In-Class Meeting

### Scope Discussion

- Discussed whether the project is sufficiently "social" and identified the need to confirm this with the tutor.
- Considered involving both partners directly in the platform. However, couples may prefer to keep their problems private rather than share them with others. This is an assumption that could be tested in the next round of interviews.
- Based on Rosti's overview, agreed to narrow the project scope.
- Decided to focus on people who **want to communicate**.
- Identified **mood** as the main project direction.

### Ideas Explored

- **Mood-sharing keychain:** A couple could share their current mood, even when it is unrelated to their partner. For example, if one person has had a difficult day, their partner would know they are tired and could respond appropriately instead of taking their behaviour personally.
- **Supporting communication:** Interview findings revealed many examples of one-sided communication, such as a partner not listening. This suggests an opportunity to support communication between partners.
- **Communication and "why" gap:** A person may recognise that their partner is upset through facial expressions but not understand why.
- Explore existing communication tools.
- Investigate participants' feelings more deeply through interviews, particularly around mood detection.
- Explore ways to "soften emotions" and make it easier to approach difficult conversations.
- Use AI-generated optional questions to help users process and understand their moods while keeping the experience user-controlled.
- Track frustration and whether the underlying issue is acknowledged or addressed.

### Rosti's Feedback

- Consider the concepts of **awareness, traces, cooperation, and communication**, regardless of whether the final outcome is an app.
- Keep the experience requirements **implementation-agnostic**.
- Explore new ways to design the experience and aim for a more innovative outcome.
- Consider the situation from a **third-person perspective**, as previously discussed as a potential design opportunity.
- Start with a scenario and consider how the system could enhance the way people approach it, rather than necessarily trying to fix it.
- Focus on demonstrating the **process and experience** within the social and mobile computing space, rather than only presenting a polished final product.
