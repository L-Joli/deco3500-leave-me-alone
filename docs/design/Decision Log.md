| D021 | 18/10/26 | Product named **Feelio**, tagline "Small signals for closer connections." | D022 | 18/10/26 | Final emotion set expanded to **9 emotions** (incl. sad, tired, excited, cute, overwhelmed, stressed, happy, angry, shy), each paired with a short preset message rather than the emotion alone | Pairing emotion with a short message helps the receiving partner decide how/when to approach them (Fuks et al., 2008); testing showed emotion labels alone lacked enough context (Interview Round 3) | Supersedes the original 6-mood set from D010 | Team | Active |
| D023 | 18/10/26 | Notification approach finalised: a subtle light signal rather than an active phone notification/ping | Phone notifications have been criticised for disrupting close relationships and enabling "phubbing"-related conflict (Olsson et al., 2020; Sakel et al., 2025); a charm-based light shares the signal without requiring the phone to be opened | Resolves the open "ping vs. no ping" question raised with Trevor on 15/10 | Team | Active |
| D024 | 18/10/26 | Added a physical privacy cover over the charm's screen, so emotion updates aren't visible to people nearby unless actively checked | Emotional states are personal; covering the display keeps sharing private between partners | — | Team | Active |
| D025 | 18/10/26 | Voice-message mechanic fully replaced with supportive preset text replies (confirms and finalises the direction set in D018) | Reduces effort in finding the right words and shapes replies toward support (Olsson et al., 2020; Akrich, 1992); Interview Round 4 found participants struggled with the physical voice controls and feedback | Supersedes the voice-recording/playback mechanic from D016 | Team | Active |
| D026 | 18/10/26 | Added "matching statuses" between the pair of charms, so the sender can confirm the partner has acknowledged an update | Earlier one-way sharing risked the sender not knowing whether their partner had noticed (Strong & Gaver, 1996) | — | Team | Active |
| D027 | 18/10/26 | Research and evaluation process finalised across 4 interview rounds: Round 1 — domain exploration (6 participants); Round 2 — focused research on 5 themes (emotional awareness, uncertainty, clarification, responses, communication timing); Round 3 — initial scenario-based prototype evaluation (6 participants); Round 4 — refined prototype evaluation + survey (6 participants; 5/6 saw potential for positive communication, same number flagged possible misunderstandings) | Iterative research/testing structure used to justify and refine the final design | 3C Collaboration Model (Fuks et al., 2008) used to frame how emotional sharing supports communication and coordination between partners | Team | Active |
