# Feelio Prototype Testing Plan

## 1. Problem Statement

We are interested in supporting couples to recognise and clarify changes in each other’s emotional states during everyday interactions, so they can adjust how and when they approach one another and reduce misinterpretations that may lead to conflict.

## 2. Testing Aim

This evaluation aims to investigate whether users can understand and manipulate Feelio’s physical controls to express, receive and clarify emotional updates.

It also explores whether the emotional information provided by Feelio helps users recognise changes in their partner’s emotional state and consider adjusting how and when they approach them.

As this is an early-stage prototype, the evaluation will primarily focus on the discoverability, comprehension and usability of its physical interactions. It will secondarily explore the perceived value of Feelio in supporting emotional communication between couples.

## 3. Evaluation Objectives

The evaluation will investigate whether users can:

1.  Understand the overall purpose of Feelio.

2.  Identify the functions of its physical components.

3.  Select and send their own emotional state.

4.  Distinguish between their own and their partner’s emotions.

5.  Access their partner’s emotion by opening the belly cover.

6.  Record, confirm and send a voice message.

7.  Find and play a voice message received from their partner.

8.  Use the helmet accessory to control the public visibility of their emotion.

9.  Use emotional information to decide how and when to approach their partner.

10. Identify interactions that may cause confusion or misinterpretation.

## 4. Method

- A moderated, scenario-based usability test will be conducted using the Feelio paper prototype.

- A Wizard of Oz approach will be used to simulate selected system responses.

- Each session will take approximately 30–35 minutes.

- The evaluation will involve approximately five participants who are currently, or have previously been, in a romantic relationship.

## 5. Testing Procedure

### Stage 1: Introduction and Consent

**Estimated time: 3 minutes**

#### Facilitator says

> “Hi, thank you for participating in our prototype evaluation today.
>
> We are testing a prototype called Feelio, which is designed to support emotional communication between couples. Today, we are testing the prototype, not you. There are no right or wrong answers, and any difficulties you experience will help us improve the design.
>
> During the session, I will ask you to complete several tasks. Please think aloud by describing what you are looking at, what you think each component does, and what you expect to happen.
>
> I may remain quiet while you complete the tasks because we want to understand how you would use the prototype independently. If you become stuck, you can let me know.
>
> With your permission, we would like to take notes and audio-record this session for research purposes. Your responses will remain confidential, and you may stop the session at any time.
>
> Do you have any questions before we begin?”

### Task 1: Select and Send an Emotion

**Estimated time: 3 minutes**

#### Scenario

##### Facilitator says

> “Imagine that you have had a stressful day. Your partner does not know what has happened, but you want them to know how you are currently feeling before they contact you.”

#### Task

##### Facilitator says

> “Using Feelio, please select ‘Stressed’ as your current emotion and send it to your partner. Please remember to think aloud as you complete the task.”

#### After-task questions

##### Facilitator asks

1.  “What did you think each hand button would do?”

2.  “Was there any feedback or information you expected but did not receive?”

### Task 2: Find and Recognise the Partner’s Emotion

**Estimated time: 4 minutes**

> **Facilitator note:** This task may be conducted using a Wizard of Oz approach. Before beginning the task, prepare or simulate the partner’s current emotion on the prototype. Use the same emotion for every participant to ensure consistency across testing sessions.

#### Scenario

##### Facilitator says

> “Imagine that you and your partner had planned to spend Saturday together. You had been looking forward to it, but your partner cancelled at the last minute to spend time with their friends.
>
> You feel disappointed and want to talk to your partner about how the cancellation affected you. Before starting the conversation, you want to understand how your partner is currently feeling.”

#### Task

##### Facilitator says

> “Using Feelio, please find and identify your partner’s current emotion.
>
> Once you have identified it, please explain whether this information would affect how or when you approach your partner.”

#### After-task questions

##### Facilitator asks

1.  “What emotion do you think your partner is experiencing?”

2.  “Is the emotion alone enough to help you decide how to approach them?”

3.  “What additional information would you need before starting the conversation?”

### Task 3: Record and Send a Voice Message

**Estimated time: 4 minutes**

#### Scenario

##### Facilitator says

> “Imagine that the emotion ‘Stressed’ does not fully explain what has happened. You want to give your partner a little more context without writing a long message.”

#### Task

##### Facilitator says

> “Using Feelio, please record and send a short voice message to your partner.
>
> You may say anything you feel comfortable saying. The content of the message is not being evaluated.”

#### After-task question

##### Facilitator asks

1.  “Did this interaction feel simple or complicated? Why?”

### Task 4: Listen to a Voice Message

**Estimated time: 3–4 minutes**

> **Facilitator note:** Before beginning this task, use the Wizard of Oz approach to simulate a new voice message from the participant’s partner.

#### Scenario

##### Facilitator says

> “Imagine that your partner has sent you a voice message through Feelio.”

#### Task

##### Facilitator says

> “Using Feelio, please find and listen to the voice message.
>
> Please think aloud and describe what you are looking for, what you are trying to do, and what you expect to happen.”

#### After-task questions

##### Facilitator asks

1.  “How did you know where to find the message?”

2.  “How did you know which button would play it?”

3.  “What feedback would help you know that the message is playing, paused or finished?”

### Stage 3: Overall Post-Test Interview

**Estimated time: 5 minutes**

#### Facilitator says

> “Thank you. You have now completed all of the tasks. I would like to ask a few final questions about your overall experience.”

#### Facilitator asks

1.  “Do you think Feelio could influence how or when you approach your partner?”

2.  “Which feature felt unnecessary or too complicated?”

3.  “If you could change one thing about Feelio, what would it be?”
