# Thematic Analysis

## Summary of findings

### 1. Interaction struggles

- **Head-bop confirm not discovered or forgotten (C1, C2).** Participants expected tapping an option on screen to confirm it. One compared it to forgetting the side buttons on a phone. It was not clear that the whole head presses down; participants said it should pop out more, and one had to lean over to press it.

- **Long-press to view replies never found (C1, C2).** No participant discovered it unprompted. On a screen, participants expected a single tap. Because of this, the device appeared buggy: one participant thought the partner's reply did not match, when the screen was actually showing their own sent message.

- **Non-interactive elements looked tappable (C1).** An element with a shadow or highlight read as a button and was tapped repeatedly.

- **Unclear split between physical and screen controls (C1, C2).** Participants were unsure which actions used buttons and which used the screen. One assumed holding the button would start a video call.

- **Onboarding shaped understanding (C2, C3).** When instructions were skipped, participants got lost. C3 assumed an ongoing conversation was happening inside the device.

### 2. Conversation flow

- **Simultaneous sends were confusing (C1).** Both partners shared a mood at the same time. One only saw the reply and did not realise the partner had also shared their own mood. A separate view for "my mood + their reply" and "their mood + my reply" was suggested.

- **No clear sent confirmation (C2).** Participants were unsure whether a message had gone through.

- **Wish to reply further (C2, C3).** C2 tried to reply to a reply; C3 expected to respond to the partner's message.

- **Light history wanted, not a full log (C1, C2).** Showing the last 2–3 exchanges was suggested as a quick refresher when busy.

### 3. Message and response options

- **Sender messages skew negative (C2).** Three of four ("I'd like to talk", "Can we talk later?", "A little space, please") suggest something is wrong in the relationship. Only "Just sharing" is neutral-positive. Participants wanted options such as "thinking of you" or "sending something nice".

- **Some responses mismatch the message (C1).** "Virtual hug" in response to "I'd like to talk" can feel dismissive. Suggested alternatives: "I'm here when you need me" or "Sorry, a bit busy right now".

- **"A little space, please" reads as serious (C1).** Its responses lack reassurance, such as "I'll get back to you later". "No rush" was liked.

- **All responses are sweet; no gentle decline (C1).** Ignoring was the only alternative and was jokingly called rude. Whether a partner would read a blunt response as a joke depends on the relationship.

- **Presets valued for speed (C1).** One-tap responses were seen as convenient and time-saving.

- **Custom input requested (C3).** Typing or voice input for a personal response was suggested.

- **Few options overall (C2).** Four messages was seen as limiting.

### 4. Mood and emotion

- **Simple faces work as a conversation opener (C3).** Emotions can feel complex, but a simple face acts as a trigger to start talking. Seen as both a limit and a strength, and judged effective overall.

- **"Overwhelmed" chosen naturally (C2)** in the stress scenario.

- **Fire/intense emotion felt "too big" (C2).** The transcript is unclear; the high-intensity end may be overstated.

- **Relationship stage affects comfort (C1).** Some messages may feel uncomfortable early in a relationship but fine for established couples.

- **Risk of misread tone (C1).** A mismatched or blunt response could cause misunderstanding.

### 5. Positives

- **Cute, fun and sweet (C1, C2, C3).** Receiving a mood drew a "so sweet" reaction.

- **Low-stakes, no phone needed (C2).** Expressing a feeling without opening a phone and texting was the strongest perceived value.

- **Compared to existing products (C2)**, such as a mood watch that changes colour when a partner thinks of you.

- **Labels mostly clear (C1)**, and "No rush" well liked.

- **Screen size fine for viewing (C2).**

- **Willingness to use (C3).** Both male participants said they would use it, with conditions on appearance.

### 6. Other considerations

- **Appearance too cute for some men (C3).** One participant suggested swappable shells (e.g. a small car, Star Wars style) with the same internals; the helmet accessory was still seen as too cute. The other participant did not mind the look, as communication is the main purpose. With C1 and C2 mostly female, male perspectives are under-represented.

- **Battery and offline states (C3).** If a partner's device is dead, silence could be read as disinterest. Battery life was asked about directly.

- **Noticing new messages.** One participant would keep the keychain inside a bag. How a new mood is noticed (vibration, light, sound) was not tested.

- **Spamming (C1)** was briefly raised as a concern.

- **Session consistency.** Instructions varied across sessions, the screen was off at one point, and replying appeared possible in some sessions but not C3 ("2 types" mentioned).

## Findings to next steps

Two interaction issues will be changed now; five further items are for team discussion before any change.

### Changes to make

| Finding                                                                            | Change                         | Possible directions                                                                                                                                                |
|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Long-press to view reply was never discovered and looked like a bug (C1, C2)       | Remove long-press              | Show the reply directly on the main view; or use a single tap/button to toggle between sent and received                                                           |
| Participants did not know when to press the physical button vs the screen (C1, C2) | Clarify button vs screen roles | One consistent rule (e.g. screen shows, button acts); remove shadows from non-interactive screen elements; or show a short on-screen hint for which control to use |

### To consider

| Finding                                                                                                       | Consideration                                       | Possible directions                                                                                       |
|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Head-bop confirm was missed or forgotten; the head did not clearly read as a button (C1, C2)                  | Improve the button affordance on the head           | Make the head visibly raised with clear press travel (Bop It style); or a separate, obvious button on top |
| Message memory raised several times; when both partners send at once, messages disappear immediately (C1, C2) | Keep recent messages visible                        | Show the last 2–3 exchanges; separate "You" and "Partner" areas so simultaneous sends are not overwritten |
| "A little space, please" reads as too serious (C1)                                                            | Change the message tone                             | Replace with the participant's suggestion: "I'll get back to you later" (or "I'll text you later")        |
| Too few message options; 3 of 4 imply a relationship problem (C2)                                             | Add more options, keeping them positive             | e.g. "Thinking of you", "Miss you", "Proud of you", "Sending you something nice"                          |
| Partner's dead battery could be read as disinterest (C3)                                                      | Partner battery visibility, weighed against privacy | Show a simple "partner offline" or low-battery status; or make battery sharing opt-in for both partners   |
