## 1. The Current Situation Is...
*What's the problem today, before this design exists?*

People experiencing recurring boundary-related discomfort in a romantic relationship often have no private space to first clarify their own feelings, before facing the pressure of deciding whether to raise it with their partner — or they choose to stay silent long-term instead.

## 2. The Stakeholders Are...
*Who has an interest in, or is affected by, this design — both direct and indirect?*

- **Direct:** the user; the user's partner 
- **Indirect:** the design team; future maintainers; interview participants

## 3. Our Design Intentions Are...
*What do you want to change in the current situation, and why?*

To let users privately recognise and reflect on recurring boundary-related discomfort in their relationship, so they can clarify their own feelings and boundaries before deciding whether — and how — to discuss it with their partner.

---

## 4. Unethical Situations With Our Design...
*Imagine the design in use. What dark, wrong, or weird situations could arise? What if it's used over a long period, by the "wrong" people, in a different context, or combined with other tech? Who can access it — what if it's hacked? Brainstorm freely as a team; use sticky notes to speed things up.*

**Draft starting points**
1. Usage records get seen by a partner, turning private reflection into conflict or "evidence" against the user
2. The tool lets someone keep privately journaling indefinitely instead of ever having the conversation — enabling avoidance
3. A controlling partner uses it to monitor or interrogate the other person
4. A data leak exposes the user's relationship situation
5. The tool's prompts lead a user to misjudge something as an "unreasonable boundary" when it's really a cultural or situational difference
