# Interview Transcript

**Interviewer:**

Hi, thank you for taking the time to participate in this interview today. We’re exploring how people in romantic relationships notice and respond to each other’s emotional states.

So, can you walk me through a recent everyday moment—maybe yesterday or last week—when you could tell your partner was in a particular mood before either of you said anything about it?

**Interviewee:**

Yeah, I think I can usually notice my partner’s mood. Whenever his face looks different, I usually just ask how he’s feeling. Like if he has a poker face or something, or I can just feel what he’s feeling right now, I’ll ask him, “Are you feeling tired now?” or “Are you not in a good mood? Why are you not in a good mood?” Something like that.

**Interviewer:**

Thinking about a moment like that recently, what were the actual signals that tipped you off? Like, was it tone of voice, body language, silence, or something else?

**Interviewee:**

Hmm, what do you mean by “tipped me off”?

**Interviewer:**

What was the first signal that made you think, “Hey, he’s not in his normal mood”? What made you first identify that he wasn’t being his usual self?

**Interviewee:**

I think it would be his facial expression. It’s not like the way he usually is, so I would kind of get that signal.

**Interviewer:**

And once you noticed that, did it change how you approached him or what you did next?

**Interviewee:**

Once I notice, I usually just ask him about how he feels right now. I mean, I would directly ask him, “Are you feeling tired?” or “Are you feeling sad?” or whatever.

**Interviewer:**

And has there been a time when you got that wrong—when you thought he was in one mood, but it turned out that wasn’t right? What happened, and how did you realise you’d got it wrong?

**Interviewee:**

Oh yeah, definitely. After I asked him, he said he wasn’t actually sad or anything. Maybe he was just tired, or maybe he just wanted to put on his poker face. It doesn’t mean he’s really in a negative mood at that moment.

**Interviewer:**

How do you usually let him know how you’re feeling? Do you say it outright, show it through how you’re acting, or just wait for him to notice? And why do you do it that way?

**Interviewee:**

I think it depends. Sometimes I’ll just directly let him know what I’m feeling right now, but sometimes I would also like it if he could notice before I say it out loud.

**Interviewer:**

Why’s that?

**Interviewee:**

Maybe at that time I’m just too tired to say it, or because of my feelings. Sometimes maybe he did something that really pissed me off, and it’s not the first time. Then I just want him to notice, “Oh, I did something wrong,” instead of me explicitly saying, “I’m really mad about that.”

**Interviewer:**

And when something like that happens—when one of you has misread how the other one is feeling—what tends to happen next?

**Interviewee:**

It depends too. Sometimes if I misread his feelings, but I ask him about his feelings first, then we’re usually cool with that. But if we kind of misread each other and we put on some mood, like maybe I directly say, “Why are you in this mood?”, then sometimes we will argue.

**Interviewer:**

Now thinking about something a bit more difficult or sensitive that you felt you needed to bring up, how do you judge whether your partner is in the right state for that sort of conversation? Are there particular signals you look for to decide whether now is the right time or not?

**Interviewee:**

Like, what’s the right time to say something?

Yeah. If there’s something difficult to talk about, or something sensitive that you want to bring up, how do you judge when the moment is right to have that conversation?

**Interviewee:**

It’s actually pretty hard. Sometimes I try to find the right time, but it’s never the right time. So when I try to talk about something more sensitive, maybe I talk in the wrong way, or it just depends on how he feels as well.

**Interviewer:**

Has there been a time when you held back just because you were cautious about how your partner seemed to be?

**Interviewee:**

Yeah, I did. I can tell maybe he’s not in a good mood, so I probably would just pause and wait until next time.

**Interviewer:**

And what went through your head at that point?

**Interviewee:**

I just think it’s not a good time to discuss because he’s not in a good mood. If I bring it up now, maybe we’ll get into conflict again.

I’m not sure. Maybe each of us sometimes should let each other know about our emotional state.

**Interviewer:**

So you’re thinking more openness from both sides rather than guessing?

**Interviewee:**

Yeah, kind of.

Is there anything else you wish was easier in those situations?

In situations where emotions are running high and it’s difficult to work out what the other person is thinking.

Oh, I think sometimes I wish there was someone who could tell me what to say and when a good time is to talk about things.
