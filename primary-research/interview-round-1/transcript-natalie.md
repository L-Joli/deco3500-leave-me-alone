# Interview Transcript

**Interview topic:** Boundaries in romantic relationships  
**Participants:** Interviewer and Participant

This interview aims to understand boundaries within romantic relationships. Let’s begin with the first question.

## Question 1: Behaviors or situations that cause discomfort

**Interviewer:**  
Are there any behaviors or situations that would make you feel uncomfortable in a relationship? Why would they make you uncomfortable?

**Participant:**  
For example, I would feel uncomfortable if someone of the opposite sex asked my partner to go out alone with them or wanted to talk to him about personal or emotional issues.

The other person might already be crossing a boundary. However, if my partner also did not clearly communicate that he was already in a relationship and that meeting them might be inappropriate, I would feel that he was not maintaining appropriate boundaries either. For example, he could say, “I have a girlfriend, so I might not be the right person to help you with this.”

**Interviewer:**  
So, if someone of the opposite sex asked your partner to go out alone and they both treated it as completely normal without acknowledging the relationship, you would feel uncomfortable. Could you explain what “having no sense of boundaries” means to you?

**Participant:**  
It means that the person does not recognise that my partner and I are in a relationship. I think that when two people are together, it is almost as if there is a circle around them. Both partners should maintain an appropriate distance from other people, regardless of whether those people are single.

My partner should also avoid interfering in someone else’s relationship. For example, I would not be comfortable with him asking a woman who already has a boyfriend to go out alone with him.

**Interviewer:**  
So, it is mainly about the level of closeness between your partner and other people.

**Participant:**  
Yes. They should maintain some distance. It does not mean they cannot interact at all, but there should be limits on how close they become.

**Interviewer:**  
Could you explain where you would draw that boundary? How close would be too close?

**Participant:**  
For example, if someone said to my partner, “I’m feeling upset. Can you come out and have a late-night meal with me?” I would consider that too intimate.

However, if there were a weekend picnic with a large group of people and my boyfriend was also going, that would be fine. It would simply be a group of friends attending an activity together.

## Question 2: Experiences of communicating a boundary or concern

**Interviewer:**  
Can you tell me about a time when you wanted to communicate a boundary or concern to a partner?

**Participant:**  
I recently saw an example involving a couple online. It was presented from the boyfriend’s perspective. Another woman sent him a message saying she was feeling upset and asked whether he could meet her. She also asked whether he wanted to see a movie with her the following week.

The man replied, “Sure. Can I bring my girlfriend so that you can meet her?” The other woman responded, “Oh, really? But she might be hostile towards me.”

I think this clearly illustrates the issue. The man actively maintained a boundary and clearly told the other woman that he had a girlfriend. However, the other woman clearly did not respect that boundary.

**Interviewer:**  
So, your understanding of boundaries is mainly related to the appropriate level of closeness between people. Have you personally experienced a similar situation?

**Participant:**  
I have not been in a relationship for a long time. Thinking back, I believe my former partner and I were both quite good at maintaining boundaries. Neither of us actively tried to meet new people who could potentially become romantic interests. We also would not accept certain invitations, such as invitations to go clubbing.

**Interviewer:**  
So, you both maintained appropriate boundaries in the relationship?

**Participant:**  
Yes, I think we both managed that quite well.

## Another experience of expressing discomfort

**Interviewer:**  
Can you think of a time when your partner did something that made you uncomfortable and you wanted to express your feelings to him? It does not necessarily have to involve a third party or relationship boundaries. It could be any situation in which you disagreed.

**Participant:**  
We had an argument once because he wanted to introduce me to a group of his childhood friends. There were four of them, and they had known each other for a long time. They were almost like childhood companions.

The first time he invited me, I did not really want to go, so I told him, “You can go without me.” He invited me again later, but I declined the second invitation as well.

One day, while we were arguing about something else, he suddenly brought it up. He said something like, “This is just the kind of person you are. You’re not even willing to meet the friends I grew up with, even though they are like family to me.” I felt quite hurt when he said that.

I simply did not feel like having a meal with them at the time. They were also older than me, which made me feel pressured. I was only in my second year of university. Although I am generally an outgoing person, I just did not feel comfortable meeting them at that time.

**Interviewer:**  
Did you communicate your thoughts or feelings to him afterwards?

**Participant:**  
Yes. I told him immediately, “The way you said that made me feel uncomfortable. I actually feel quite hurt.” We had initially been arguing about something else, but he suddenly brought up this unrelated issue.

**Interviewer:**  
How did you eventually resolve the situation?

**Participant:**  
I do not think we really resolved it because we broke up not long afterwards.

However, we did agree that the next time I visited Taipei, I could have a meal with his friends. If it was genuinely important to him that I meet the people who were important in his life, I was willing to do that. So, we did reach a relatively clear conclusion.

## Question 3: Distinguishing an isolated incident from an ongoing problem

**Interviewer:**  
When something uncomfortable happens in a relationship, how do you usually decide whether it is an isolated incident or something that requires further attention?

**Participant:**  
If the same issue is brought up more than once, I would consider it something that requires attention.

For example, Friend A and her boyfriend have an ongoing disagreement. If the same issue is mentioned repeatedly, you know that they may continue arguing about it for a long time because they have never reached a conclusion.

Her boyfriend likes staying at home, whereas Friend A wants to go out on weekends. They have never reached an agreement about it, so it has become a recurring problem.

**Interviewer:**  
So, you mainly judge it according to how frequently the issue occurs or is discussed?

**Participant:**  
Yes. If the first argument ends without a conclusion and the second discussion also ends without a conclusion, it may become an ongoing problem.

**Interviewer:**  
Apart from how often it happens, are there any other factors you consider?

**Participant:**  
It could also relate to differences in the partners’ family backgrounds or personalities.

For example, I was still a student at the time and did not have much money, while my former boyfriend came from a wealthier family. We once discussed how we did not need to eat at expensive restaurants every time we went out. We could eat at more affordable places instead.

After we discussed it, we both agreed, and the situation improved. Therefore, I would consider that an isolated issue. I cannot currently think of an ongoing issue from that relationship because it happened about five years ago.

## Question 4: Managing unwanted or uncomfortable communication

**Interviewer:**  
How do you usually respond when someone communicates with you in an unwanted way or says something that makes you uncomfortable?

**Participant:**  
I would address it directly. Like in the argument I mentioned earlier, if I feel uncomfortable, I will say it immediately instead of keeping my feelings to myself.

I would express how I feel and let the other person know, “The way you said that hurt me.”

I would also explain that there may be a better way to communicate what they want. For example, he could have said, “I hope you can join our next gathering,” instead of criticising or blaming me.

**Interviewer:**  
Do you communicate your feelings directly because of your habits, values or another reason?

**Participant:**  
Of course, the starting point is that I feel uncomfortable. I also do not think I am someone who tends to overthink things internally. I would rather discuss the issue directly and ask, “Why did you say it that way?” or “You could have expressed yourself in a better way, so why did you choose such harsh words?”

**Interviewer:**  
So, in a relationship, you tend to discuss an issue directly with your partner when something happens?

**Participant:**  
Yes. I am not sure whether I always did this in the past, but now, if someone makes me feel uncomfortable, I will tell them directly.

## Question 5: Helpful support when facing a difficult situation

**Interviewer:**  
When you are dealing with a particularly difficult situation, what kind of support would be most helpful to you? It does not necessarily have to be support from your partner. It could be any form of support.

**Participant:**  
I would go for a walk by myself, watch a movie, go for a run or exercise at the gym. I think exercise is a good way to immediately relieve feelings of anger or sadness.

**Interviewer:**  
After you have calmed down, what would you do if the problem still remained?

**Participant:**  
When I have an argument, I think I am the kind of person who needs to reach a conclusion. If we decide to discuss the issue, we need to reach some kind of conclusion. We cannot simply leave it unresolved or both pretend that the problem does not exist.

However, if it is an ongoing issue like the one between Friend A and her boyfriend—something that may be fundamentally impossible to resolve—then one person may eventually need to compromise.

For example, one person may want to study overseas while the other person is unable to move overseas, so they would need to discuss having a long-distance relationship. Fundamental differences like that cannot always be fully resolved.

**Interviewer:**  
When you encounter an issue that may not be resolved, would you still hope to find a solution, or would you accept the situation as it is?

**Participant:**  
Of course, I would hope that it could be resolved. Finding a solution would always be the best outcome. Issues that genuinely cannot be resolved are usually very difficult ones. I would not want to simply ignore the issue or pretend that it does not exist.

**Interviewer:**  
If the issue were particularly difficult to manage, what kind of help or support would you want?

**Participant:**  
I would want my partner to be as concerned about the issue as I am and to show that he also wants to resolve it.

Even if we ultimately could not find a solution, it would help to know that he genuinely wanted to work on the issue. If we both acknowledged the problem and had a shared understanding of it, the situation would feel more manageable. I do not necessarily need a specific form of support.

**Interviewer:**  
If your partner did not share the same understanding, how would you communicate your concerns to him?

**Participant:**  
I would say, “If we genuinely cannot resolve this issue, what are we going to do?”

If neither of us knew how to resolve it at that moment and neither of us could suggest a suitable solution, that could still be considered a shared understanding. We would both recognise that we did not currently have a solution.

## Question 6: Other important issues relating to boundaries

**Interviewer:**  
Is there anything about interpersonal boundaries or difficult situations in relationships that we have not discussed but that you think is important?

**Participant:**  
I would want both of us to maintain appropriate boundaries. I would not want someone who does not understand boundaries to interfere in our relationship or enter the space between us.

I do not think I would choose a partner who had no understanding of boundaries. I would be more concerned about someone suddenly appearing, failing to respect those boundaries and attempting to interfere in our relationship.

## Assessing whether a partner understands boundaries

**Interviewer:**  
How would you assess whether a potential partner understands and respects boundaries?

**Participant:**  
I think you can observe their previous relationship experiences. If their previous relationships lasted for a relatively long time, I might feel that they were more trustworthy and that they approached relationships seriously and responsibly.

For example, if each of their relationships lasted about three years, I might see that as a positive sign. However, it would not necessarily guarantee anything.

**Interviewer:**  
Apart from their previous experiences, once you are in a relationship with someone, how would you determine whether they understand boundaries?

**Participant:**  
You can observe how they talk about other couples. When discussing couples they know, they might say, “They should have done this,” or “They should not have done that.” Their comments can reveal their values and beliefs about romantic relationships.

Alternatively, I could share an example involving another couple and listen to how my partner responds. His comments could help me understand his relationship values.

## Responding to a third party who crosses boundaries

**Interviewer:**  
If someone outside the relationship did not respect your boundaries, how would you deal with that person or situation?

**Participant:**  
I would first clarify how my boyfriend felt about that person and what he planned to do.

If I could confirm that my boyfriend was maintaining appropriate boundaries, I would feel reassured because I would know that he would not allow the other person to interfere in our relationship.

**Interviewer:**  
How would you confirm whether he was maintaining appropriate boundaries? Would you consider his values and previous experiences, or would you ask him specific questions?

**Participant:**  
I would observe whether he made it clear during the conversation that meeting the other person would be inappropriate, or whether he mentioned that he was already in a relationship.

He would not necessarily need to reject the person very directly. He could say something like, “I’ll introduce you to my partner next time.” I think that sentence would clearly demonstrate that he understands and is maintaining the boundary.

In other words, he could naturally mention his partner during the conversation. For example, he could say, “I’ll bring my boyfriend so you can meet him next time.” That would clearly communicate the boundary. If my boyfriend responded in that way, I would feel reassured.

**Interviewer:**  
What if he did not respond that way?

**Participant:**  
If he did not make the boundary clear, I would ask him why. I would explain which behaviours made me uncomfortable and tell him, “Please maintain an appropriate distance from that person in the future because boundaries within a relationship are important to me.”

**Interviewer:**  
So, you would communicate your feelings and explain your boundaries to him?

**Participant:**  
Yes. I think it is essential to communicate them.
